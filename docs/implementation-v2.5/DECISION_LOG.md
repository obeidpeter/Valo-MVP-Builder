## Decision lifecycle

- A small, reversible implementation choice stays in its pull request. A change to an invariant, architecture driver, deployable/module direction, public or high-risk route policy, tenant/identity boundary, provider/workload activation, release protocol, irreversible migration or accepted risk requires an ADR or an amendment to the owning ADR.
- Every ADR records `Status`, `Date`, owner, backup owner, required reviewers, linked `AD-*` drivers, source evidence, last/next review, and supersession. `Superseded by` names an ADR only after the predecessor status becomes `Superseded`; the successor names it in `Supersedes`.
- `Accepted` means the decision is authoritative. Its metadata must still state whether source, deployment and verification evidence exist; acceptance never upgrades missing operational evidence.
- Review is due on the ADR's `Next review` date and immediately after a material incident, production topology/provider change, driver/risk change or contradictory implementation. Reviewers update the decision, evidence links, catalogues and affected current/target views together.
- Catalogue `sourceReviewedThrough` dates, this log's `Source reviewed through` date and affected architecture-view review dates advance together only after source reconciliation; a newer catalogue makes stale architecture views fail repository verification.
- The accountable owner cannot satisfy an independent security, privacy or production-review requirement alone. Until a backup is assigned, that governance gap remains explicit as `AR-001`.
